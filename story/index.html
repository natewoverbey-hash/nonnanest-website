Our Story

I built the monitor I couldn't find.

Nonnanest exists because one night I needed a monitor that didn't exist yet. Everything you see on this site started with that moment, and the two years of quiet work that came after it.

Nathan Overbey Founder & CEO Customer Zero

January 2023. 3 AM. My son Reed's skin was warmer than it should have been and the monitor next to my bed showed nothing. That was the night the gap became obvious. And it's the night Nonnanest started.

The gap I kept running into

I have a daughter, my firstborn, and a son, Reed. The idea for Nonnanest started with Reed, and that night in January.

In the days after, I started researching how to solve the specific problem I'd had. Skin-temperature awareness. From across the room. Without picking him up.

The baby monitor market has a lot of products. Wearables. WiFi cameras. Cameras with AI alerts. Some interesting design work happening around the edges, and a few brands genuinely innovating. But most of the category is static. Most "new" products are variations on the same idea. Another camera. Another wearable. Another app.

The WiFi cameras were the most interesting to me. I liked the idea of being able to check on my baby when I was away for a night, or when we had a sitter. That's a real thing parents want. But even the interesting products weren't solving for skin-temperature awareness. Nobody was building it.

So I built it.

Why "another camera" wasn't the answer

The market was full of cameras. Some of them were great cameras. That was the whole point. If I already had a good camera, adding a slightly better one wasn't going to solve the January problem.

My problem wasn't that I couldn't see Reed. My problem was that I couldn't sense what was happening with him without picking him up. That's a wellness problem, not a video problem. And nobody in the category was treating it that way.

So I stopped looking for another camera and started asking a different question. What would a monitor look like if it was designed to add calm instead of features?

The best kind of monitoring is the kind your baby never feels.

How this actually happened

I've spent fifteen years in enterprise software sales. I have an MBA. I do see problems and think "somebody should build this." Usually the somebody is already out there and I just have to find them. That's what I'd tried to do with the January problem.

This time there was no somebody.

I started talking to other parents. Not to sell anything. Nobody was selling anything yet. Just to check if the gap I'd hit was mine, or if other parents were hitting the same one. It was clear pretty quickly. Everyone had a version of the same story, and nobody had a product that answered it. That's a gap, and a need, and an opportunity.

That's when I stopped looking for a product and started building one.

By March of 2023 I'd started looking for a manufacturing partner. By August of 2023 I had one, and that's when the real build started. A small team of engineers who understood contactless infrared sensing and closed-network wireless. I patented the mechanism. We sourced a screen that would live on a bedside table and not on a phone. We ran the prototype through the awards program that judges baby products the way parents actually use them, because I wanted to hear from real evaluators before I told anyone else this was real.

Every design decision was measured against a single question. Does this add calm, or does this add noise? If it added noise, it didn't ship. That's why there's no WiFi in the box, no app to download, no account to create, and no subscription to keep alive.

We built it at a slower pace on purpose. A pace that let us test the product on our own kids first. A pace that let us have real conversations with real parents before we tried to sell them anything. A pace that let us say "not yet" more often than "ship it."

What "wellness, on purpose" means

Nonnanest is a wellness monitor. That is the honest description of what it does. It supports your awareness of your baby's comfort. It does not diagnose anything. It is not intended to replace a contact thermometer or a call to your pediatrician when your gut says something is off.

We could have tried to be something else. There is a whole category of consumer products that market themselves in medical language. I have watched some of them get called on it. I did not want to build one of those, and I did not want to sell one to another parent.

"Wellness, on purpose" is the shortest way I know how to say that. It means: I built this to support your instinct, not to compete with your pediatrician's judgment.

What Customer Zero means going forward

I used this on my own kids before I let anyone else buy one. That's what Customer Zero has meant so far. It also means something for what comes next.

Every design decision we make about Nonnanest, from now until the day the company stops existing, has to pass the same test the first version passed. Would I be comfortable putting it in my own kid's nursery. Would I be proud to describe it, in plain language, to another parent at a pediatrician's office. Would I want it as it is, or would I be waiting for it to get better before I recommended it to a friend.

If a change to the product would fail any of those questions, we don't ship it. That's the promise. That's the whole company.
