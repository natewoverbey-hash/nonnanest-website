Nonnanest was designed to be present without being loud. The next section explains what that looks like in practice.

Four principles

What "wellness awareness" actually means here.

Not a marketing phrase. Four design decisions that shape what the device does, and — just as importantly — what it doesn't try to do.

01 · SKIN TEMPERATURE

The signal your thermometer doesn't show you.

SightAware reads the actual temperature at your baby's skin — from above the crib, without touching them. Your ear or forehead thermometer adds a mathematical offset to estimate a deeper reading. We don't do that. We show what's actually happening on the surface, where skin is quietly trading heat with the room to keep your baby comfortable.

Why it matters: a mid-90s read on SightAware isn't a "low" reading. It's a healthy one for a sleeping baby. When it starts to climb, that's the signal — and that's the moment to reach for a contact thermometer.
02 · YOUR BABY'S BASELINE, NOT A GENERIC THRESHOLD

What "normal" looks like is different for every baby.

SightAware doesn't ship with a preset alert. You watch what the reading looks like when your baby is comfortable and well for a few nights, and that becomes your baseline. Then you set an alert threshold above it — usually 2 to 3°F above the top of that baseline. That's intentional. No two babies read the same, and a pre-set threshold designed for the average child would be too tight or too loose for most.

Why it matters: a personalized baseline surfaces real change; a generic threshold surfaces false alarms.
03 · AWARENESS, NOT INTERVENTION

The device prompts a check. It doesn't answer the question.

When an alert fires, that's the cue to check your baby with a contact thermometer, look at them, and — if your gut says something is off — call your pediatrician. SightAware supports the check. It doesn't replace it. Every design decision on the handheld reflects that: no diagnosis language, no confidence scores, no push toward action that isn't yours to take.

Why it matters: awareness devices earn trust by respecting the parent's judgment. Alarm devices erode it — one false alarm at a time.
04 · A WELLNESS MONITOR, ON PURPOSE

Not trying to be something we're not.

Nonnanest is designed as a wellness monitor. That's a category that supports parents' general awareness of a baby's comfort — not a device that tries to replace clinical judgment. The framing is intentional. It's also the honest description of what a contactless skin-temperature read can and can't tell you. What lives on your bedside table should support your instinct — not compete with your pediatrician.

Why it matters: you get a signal you can trust in its lane. Not a false sense of certainty about anything outside it.

Where wellness awareness starts — and where it's going

Insight should broaden with your baby, not with your worry.

We designed Nonnanest to be honest about what a monitor can and can't know. The signal we ship today is deliberately narrow. What we're exploring next expands the awareness picture — without expanding what the device claims to be.

Today

Contactless skin-temperature awareness.

A quiet read from above the crib, personalized to your baby's baseline, with an alert threshold you set. Live view on the dedicated 5" display. No wearable. No app. No cloud. The signal is narrow on purpose — it's the one we can deliver honestly, without overreaching what a wellness device is meant to do.

What this meansYou know what "normal" looks like for your baby. When something changes, you know to check.
Where we're going

A fuller picture of the nursery — without adding noise.

Wellness awareness is more than temperature. Room conditions, sleep environment, patterns over time — these are things a well-designed nursery monitor can help parents see, if the design bar is "notice the change" rather than "trigger the alarm." We're exploring which of these signals belong on a wellness device, and which don't. What ships next will be what we can deliver without medicalizing the experience.

What this meansBroader awareness, same restraint. If a signal can't help without over-claiming, it doesn't ship.
How we think about this

Awareness that respects your judgment.

The most valuable insight we can give a parent isn't a diagnosis — it's a well-timed prompt to look at their baby. Every design decision on Nonnanest, today and going forward, is measured against that. A signal that would push a parent toward panic instead of a check doesn't earn a place on the device. A quiet, honest one does.

What this meansWellness insights are only useful if they make parenting feel calmer, not more surveilled.

What this page is not saying.

We're not saying skin-temperature awareness is a substitute for a contact thermometer or a call to your pediatrician. It isn't. It's designed to help you notice the change that prompts one of those checks — not to make the check for you.

What we're saying is that a wellness monitor, thoughtfully designed, can quietly surface the moments that deserve a closer look — without waking anyone, without a wearable on your baby, and without pretending to know more than it does. That's the specific job Nonnanest was built for.

What Nonnanest does
Contactless awareness of your baby's skin temperature · a personalized baseline that reflects your baby, not an average · a plain-English signal that prompts you to check
What Nonnanest doesn't do
Replace a contact thermometer · replace a call to your pediatrician · make decisions that are yours to make · substitute for a parent's instinct

When to call your pediatrician.

Trust your gut. If your baby seems uncomfortable, isn't feeding well, is unusually sleepy or fussy, or if a contact thermometer reading concerns you — call your pediatrician. Nonnanest supports your awareness. It doesn't replace it.
